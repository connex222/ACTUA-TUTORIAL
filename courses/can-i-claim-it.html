    Lesson 1 of 7

    The golden rule

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The test every expense must pass
    • Why claiming matters

    If you’re self-employed, you pay tax on your profit: your business income minus your allowable expenses. Every genuine cost you claim lowers your profit, and so your tax and National Insurance.

    Wholly and exclusively

    To be allowable, a cost must be spent wholly and exclusively for the purposes of your business. If something has a personal side as well, you can usually claim only the business part, and some things can’t be claimed at all.

    Example

    Jo, a basic-rate taxpayer, forgets to claim £500 of genuine business costs. Jo pays £100 too much Income Tax (20%), plus extra National Insurance on top.

    Keep the proof

    Every claim needs evidence: a receipt, invoice or log. The Snap & Send course covers good receipt habits.

    Lesson 3 of 7

    What you can’t claim

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The costs people most often get wrong
    Not allowableWhy
    Entertaining clients, such as meals, drinks or event ticketsSpecifically blocked by tax rules
    Everyday clothes, even if only worn for workThey have a personal use too
    Fines and penalties, such as parking or speeding finesNot a cost of trading
    Commuting between home and your permanent workplaceTreated as personal travel
    Personal costs, such as your own food on a normal working dayNot for the business
    Gifts of food, drink, tobacco or vouchers to customersSpecifically blocked by tax rules
    Small branded gifts can count

    A gift to a customer can be allowed if it carries a clear advert for your business, costs no more than £50 per person in a year, and isn’t food, drink, tobacco or a voucher. Branded pens or mugs usually qualify; a Christmas hamper doesn’t.

    Travel to temporary sites

    Travelling to a customer, a temporary site or a supplier usually is allowable. It’s the regular trip to your own fixed workplace that isn’t.

    Lesson 4 of 7

    Grey areas and split-use costs

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • How to claim things used partly for business

    Many costs are used both for the business and privately. You can usually claim the business share, worked out in a fair, reasonable way that you can explain if asked.

    CostA fair way to split it
    Mobile phoneThe share of calls and data used for business, from a typical month’s bill
    Home broadbandThe share of use for business, or hours worked
    Car or van (actual costs)Business miles as a share of total miles, from a mileage log
    Home heating and powerThe rooms used for work and the hours they’re used
    Be ready to explain

    HMRC can ask how you worked out a split. Keep a note of your method and any evidence, such as a mileage log or a sample phone bill.

    Not sure?

    Ask your accountant. Grey areas are exactly what they’re for.

    Lesson 5 of 7

    Simplified expenses and the trading allowance

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Flat rates instead of working out actual costs
    • When the £1,000 allowance is better

    Sole traders and partnerships can use HMRC’s simplified expenses: flat rates for three kinds of cost instead of adding up actual bills.

    Vehicles: mileage rates

    Vehicle2025 to 20262026 to 2027 (from 6 April 2026)
    Cars and vans, first 10,000 business miles45p a mile55p a mile
    Cars and vans, after 10,000 miles25p a mile25p a mile
    Motorcycles24p a mile24p a mile
    Stick with your choice

    Once you use mileage rates for a vehicle, you must keep using them for as long as you have that vehicle. You can’t also claim its fuel, insurance or repairs. Keep a log of every business journey.

    Working from home

    Business hours worked at home each monthFlat rate a month
    25 to 50£10
    51 to 100£18
    101 or more£26

    This covers heat and power only. You can still claim the business share of phone and internet separately. Living at your business premises, such as a guest house, has its own flat-rate adjustment.

    The £1,000 trading allowance

    Instead of claiming any expenses, you can deduct a flat £1,000 from your self-employment income. It suits side businesses with very low costs. You can’t use it and claim expenses as well.

    Lesson 6 of 7

    Big purchases: equipment and vehicles

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • How equipment is claimed
    • Why cars are different

    Things you buy to keep and use in the business, such as tools, machinery, computers and vans, are usually claimed through capital allowances rather than as day-to-day expenses.

    Annual Investment Allowance

    Most equipment can be claimed in full in the year you buy it, up to £1 million a year, under the Annual Investment Allowance.

    Cars are different

    Cars don’t qualify for the Annual Investment Allowance. They’re claimed over several years at rates that depend on their CO2 emissions. Vans are treated as equipment. Ask your accountant before buying a vehicle through the business.

    Cash basis

    Most sole traders use the cash basis, where many equipment purchases can be claimed as normal expenses. Cars still follow special rules. Your accountant will know which applies to you.

    Lesson 7 of 7

    If you run a limited company

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • How the rules differ

    A limited company follows the same broad “wholly and exclusively” test, but the company pays the costs, and anything that benefits a director or employee personally can have tax consequences for them.

    TopicWhat’s different
    Paying expensesThe company pays, or reimburses you. Keep receipts and an expense claim.
    MileageThe company can pay you up to the approved mileage rate tax-free for business journeys in your own car.
    Working from homeThe company can pay you a flat amount without receipts, or a share of actual costs with evidence.
    BenefitsThings the company provides for your personal use may count as taxable benefits. Small occasional gifts may be exempt under the trivial benefits rules.
    Get it set up properly

    Your accountant can set up a simple expenses process for your company so that claims are correct and well recorded.

    Common questions

    Select a question to see the answer.

    I lost a receipt. Can I still claim?

    Try to get a copy from the supplier. If you can’t, keep a note of what you bought, when and why, with the bank statement line. Tell your accountant; without a VAT invoice, VAT normally can’t be reclaimed.

    Can I claim my gym membership?

    Usually not. It’s a personal cost, even if fitness helps your work.

    Can I claim my own wages?

    Not as a sole trader: money you take out is drawings, not an expense. A limited company can pay you a salary, which is a company expense.

    I bought a laptop I use for work and personal things

    Claim the business share. Your accountant will decide how to treat it as equipment.

    Reference

    Words explained

    Allowable expense
    A cost you can deduct from income to work out taxable profit.
    Annual Investment Allowance
    Lets most equipment be claimed in full in the year it’s bought, up to £1 million.
    Capital allowances
    Tax relief on equipment and vehicles kept and used in the business.
    Drawings
    Money a sole trader takes out for personal use; not an expense.
    Simplified expenses
    HMRC flat rates for vehicles, working from home and living at business premises.
    Trading allowance
    A flat £1,000 deduction instead of claiming actual expenses.
    Wholly and exclusively
    The test that an expense must be purely for the business.
