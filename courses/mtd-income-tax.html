    Lesson 1 of 6

    What MTD for Income Tax is

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The three things that change
    • The one thing that doesn’t

    Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax is HMRC’s new way for sole traders and landlords to keep records and report their income. It replaces the old “one tax return a year” routine for people above the income limits.

    Digital records

    Income and costs are kept in software that works with MTD, not in a notebook or loose papers.

    Quarterly updates

    Four times a year, a summary of income and costs is sent to HMRC from that software.

    Year-end tax return

    After the year ends, a tax return is still sent by 31 January to finalise everything.

    You don’t pay tax every quarter

    Quarterly updates are reports, not payments. The dates you pay Income Tax don’t change because of MTD.

    Lesson 2 of 6

    Who it applies to

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Who’s in and who’s out
    • What “qualifying income” means
    You are…MTD for Income Tax?
    A sole trader (self-employed)Yes, once your qualifying income is over the limit.
    A landlord renting out property in your own nameYes, once your qualifying income is over the limit.
    Running a limited companyNo. Companies pay Corporation Tax instead.
    A partner in a partnershipNot yet, for partnership income.

    What counts as qualifying income

    Your qualifying income is your self-employment turnover plus your rents received, added together, before any costs are taken off. If you own a rental property jointly, use your share.

    It’s turnover, not profit

    A builder with £55,000 of sales and £30,000 of costs makes a £25,000 profit, but their qualifying income is £55,000. That’s the figure that decides when MTD starts.

    Wages from a job, pensions, savings interest and dividends don’t count towards qualifying income.

    Exemptions

    If it isn’t reasonably practical for you to use digital tools, for example because of age, disability or where you live, you can apply to HMRC for an exemption. Your accountant can help you decide whether to apply.

    What you need to do

    1. Keep every sale and cost in MTD-compatible software as you go, such as Xero, QuickBooks, Sage or FreeAgent. The other courses in this series show how.
    2. Send receipts and invoices promptly (see the Snap & Send course).
    3. Each quarter, the software adds up your income and costs by category and sends a summary to HMRC.
    4. After the year ends, any adjustments are made and the year-end tax return is sent.
    Updates don’t have to be perfect

    A quarterly update is a running summary. If something was missed, it can be corrected in a later update or at the year end.

    Lesson 5 of 6

    Penalties, and the first-year easement

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • How late-submission points work
    1. Each late quarterly update or tax return earns one penalty point.
    2. Reach four points within two years and you get a £200 penalty.
    3. Paying tax late is separate, with its own penalties and interest.
    A soft first year, for the first group only

    For the 2026 to 2027 tax year, HMRC isn’t giving penalty points for late quarterly updates. This was announced for the first year of MTD, not for people who join in April 2027 or later. Don’t rely on it carrying on.

    Help

    Common worries

    Select a question to see the answer.

    Will I have to pay tax four times a year?

    No. Quarterly updates are reports. MTD doesn’t change the dates you pay Income Tax.

    I’m not good with computers

    Your accountant can do the software side for you; you just need to get your receipts and invoices to them. If using digital tools really isn’t practical for you, you may be able to apply for an exemption.

    I have a job and a small side business

    Only the side business turnover (and any rents) count towards qualifying income. Your wages don’t.

    My income went over the limit just once

    Talk to your accountant. The figure on the relevant year’s tax return decides your start date, so it’s important to look at your exact numbers.

    I own a rental property with my partner

    Use your own share of the rent when working out your qualifying income.

    Reference

    Words explained

    Digital records
    Your income and costs kept in MTD-compatible software.
    Easement
    A temporary relaxation of the rules, such as no penalty points for late quarterly updates in 2026 to 2027.
    MTD
    Making Tax Digital: HMRC’s rules for keeping digital records and reporting through software.
    Penalty point
    A mark for each late update or return. Four within two years leads to a £200 penalty.
    Qualifying income
    Self-employment turnover plus rents received, before costs.
    Quarterly update
    A summary of income and costs sent to HMRC every three months.
    Tax year
    6 April to 5 April the following year.
    Turnover
    Total sales before any costs are taken off.
