    Lesson 1 of 13

    What QuickBooks Online is

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What QuickBooks does for a business
    • The words you’ll meet in every lesson

    QuickBooks Online is accounting software that runs in your web browser and on your phone. It keeps a record of every pound that comes into and goes out of a business, so you always know where you stand.

    Get paid

    Send invoices and see who owes you money.

    Track spending

    Record bills, expenses and receipts.

    Stay compliant

    Keep digital records and send VAT returns to HMRC under Making Tax Digital.

    Words you’ll meet

    WordWhat it means
    CustomerSomeone who buys from the business.
    SupplierSomeone the business buys from.
    CategoryA label for money in or out, such as “Rent” or “Sales”. Accountants call these accounts.
    InvoiceA request for payment you send to a customer.
    BillA request for payment you receive from a supplier.
    Bank feedThe automatic link that brings your bank transactions into QuickBooks.
    Plans differ

    QuickBooks Online comes in several plans, such as Simple Start, Essentials, Plus and Advanced. Some features in this course, like bills or recurring invoices, aren’t available on every plan. If you can’t find something, your plan may not include it.

    Signing in and finding your way around

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Sign in safely
    • The four places everything lives

    Signing in

    1. Go to quickbooks.intuit.com/uk and select Sign in. Or open the QuickBooks app on your phone.
    2. Enter the email address and password for your Intuit account.
    3. Enter the security code Intuit sends you by text or email.
    4. If you have access to more than one company, pick the right one from the list.

    The four places everything lives

    PlaceWhat it’s for
    + Create (older layout: + New)The one button for making anything new: an invoice, bill, expense, payment and so on.
    Left menuYour daily work. In the new layout: Home, Feed, Reports and All apps, which opens Accounting, Sales, Expenses, Taxes and the rest.
    Search barFind a customer, supplier, amount or transaction. You can also type a page name, such as “Chart of accounts”, to jump there.
    Gear ⚙Company settings, users and other set-up options.
    Pin what you use most

    In the new layout, select Customise at the bottom of the left menu to pin pages such as Bank transactions, so they’re one click away.

    Never share your login

    Each person should have their own user. The account owner can invite people from the Gear menu, under the users section, and choose what each person can see.

    5 minutes

    In this lesson

    • Know which settings to leave alone
    1. Select the Gear ⚙, then Account and settings.
    2. On the Company tab, check the company name, address, email and phone number. These appear on your invoices.

    Lesson 4 of 13

    The chart of accounts

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What the chart of accounts is
    • The five types of account

    The chart of accounts is the full list of categories QuickBooks uses to sort your money. Every transaction ends up in one of them. Your reports are built from this list.

    To find it: select All apps, then Accounting, then Chart of accounts. Or type “Chart of accounts” into the search bar.

    TypeWhat goes in itExamples
    IncomeMoney the business earnsSales, fees
    ExpensesDay-to-day running costsRent, wages, phone
    AssetsThings the business owns, or is owedBank account, equipment, money customers owe
    LiabilitiesThings the business owesLoans, VAT owed, money owed to suppliers
    EquityThe owners’ stake in the businessShare capital, retained profits
    Don’t delete, don’t duplicate

    Ask your accountant before adding a new category. Never delete one that has been used; it can be made inactive instead. Too many similar categories (“Phone”, “Mobile”, “Phones”) make your reports hard to read.

    Lesson 5 of 13

    Connecting your bank

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Set up a bank feed
    • What to do if your bank can’t connect

    A bank feed brings your bank transactions into QuickBooks automatically, so you don’t have to type them in. QuickBooks doesn’t download transactions that are still pending at the bank.

    1. Go to Transactions (or All apps, then Banking) and select Bank transactions.
    2. Select Connect account (it may say Link account).
    3. Search for your bank and select it.
    4. You’re sent to your bank’s own secure page. Sign in there and give permission. QuickBooks never sees your banking password.
    5. Back in QuickBooks, choose which accounts to connect and how far back to bring in transactions.
    Permission needs renewing

    Your bank may ask you to renew your permission from time to time. If the feed stops bringing in new transactions, this is the most likely reason.

    Bank not listed?

    You can upload transactions from a file downloaded from your online banking instead. The file must be no bigger than 350 KB, so upload a shorter date range if it’s too large.

    Lesson 6 of 13

    Reviewing bank transactions

    6 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The three tabs on the Bank transactions page
    • When to add, when to match, when to exclude

    This is the most important daily job in QuickBooks. New transactions wait in the For review tab until you tell QuickBooks what each one is.

    1. For review holds new transactions. Categorised holds ones you’ve dealt with. Excluded holds ones you’ve removed.
    2. Add: QuickBooks has suggested a category. Check it, change it if wrong, then add it.
    3. Match: QuickBooks has found something you already entered, such as an invoice. Matching links the two, so nothing is counted twice.

    How to deal with each transaction

    1. Go to Bank transactions and pick the bank account at the top.
    2. On the For review tab, look at the first transaction.
    3. If it says Match, check the suggested record is right, then select Match. If it’s wrong, open the row and find the correct record.
    4. If it says Add (or Post), check the category and the VAT, then select Add.
    5. If one payment covers several things, open the row and use Split to divide it across categories.

    When to exclude

    Exclude a transaction only if it’s a duplicate, or it shouldn’t be in the business’s books at all. Excluded transactions don’t appear in your accounts or reports. Tick it and select Exclude. If you do it by mistake, go to the Excluded tab and select Undo.

    The most common mistake in QuickBooks

    A customer pays an invoice. The money arrives in the bank feed. If you Add it as sales income instead of Matching it to the invoice, the sale is counted twice and the invoice still shows as unpaid. Always match payments to invoices and bills.

    Personal spending on a business account

    Don’t exclude it. Ask your accountant which category to use, so it’s recorded correctly as money taken out by the owner.

    Lesson 7 of 13

    Bank rules

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What a bank rule does
    • Create a safe first rule

    A bank rule tells QuickBooks how to categorise a transaction it sees again and again. For example: “anything from BT goes to Telephone and internet”.

    1. On the Bank transactions page, open Rules (in the new layout it’s in the banking section of the left menu).
    2. Select New rule.
    3. Give the rule a clear name, such as “BT phone bill”, and choose whether it applies to money in or money out.
    4. Set the condition, such as Description contains “BT GROUP”.
    5. Choose the category, the supplier and the VAT rate to apply.
    6. Save the rule.
    Start with rules that suggest, not rules that post

    Rules can be set to add transactions automatically, without you checking them. Until you’re confident, let rules only suggest a category so you still see every transaction before it’s added. A wrong automatic rule can put months of transactions in the wrong place.

    Lesson 8 of 13

    Customers and invoices

    6 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Create and send an invoice
    • Invoice or sales receipt: which to use

    Create an invoice

    1. Select + Create (or + New), then Invoice.
    2. Choose the customer. If they’re new, select Add new and enter their name and email address.
    3. Check the invoice date and due date.
    4. On each line, pick the product or service, then enter the quantity and rate.
    5. If you’re VAT-registered, check the VAT on each line is right.
    6. Select Save and send to email it, or Save and close to send it later.
    UseWhen
    InvoiceThe customer will pay later.
    Sales receiptThe customer pays at the time of the sale.
    An invoice counts as income straight away

    QuickBooks Online records the income on your Profit and Loss report as soon as you create the invoice, not when the money arrives. That’s why the payment must be matched, not added again (lesson 9).

    Repeat invoices

    Recurring invoices can save time for regular customers, but they aren’t available on QuickBooks Online Simple Start.

    Lesson 9 of 13

    Recording customer payments

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Mark an invoice as paid
    • Avoid recording a payment twice

    There are two ways to record that a customer has paid. Use one, never both.

    Way 1: from the bank feed (recommended)

    1. When the payment appears in Bank transactions, open it.
    2. If QuickBooks shows 1 match found with the right invoice, select Match.
    3. The invoice is marked as paid and the bank is updated in one go.

    Way 2: by hand

    1. Select + Create (or + New), then Receive payment.
    2. Choose the customer.
    3. Tick the invoice they’ve paid under Outstanding transactions, and choose the payment method.
    4. Save. Later, when the money shows in the bank feed, match it to this payment.
    Part payments

    If a customer pays only part of an invoice, enter the amount they actually paid. The rest stays open on the invoice.

    Lesson 10 of 13

    Suppliers, bills and expenses

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The difference between a bill and an expense
    • Attach receipts so your records are complete
    UseWhenExample
    ExpenseYou’ve already paid.Fuel paid on the business card
    BillYou’ll pay later.A supplier invoice due in 30 days

    Enter a bill

    1. Select + Create (or + New), then Bill.
    2. Choose the supplier, then enter the bill date, due date and the supplier’s invoice number.
    3. On each line, choose the category, enter the amount and check the VAT.
    4. Attach a copy of the supplier’s invoice, then save.
    5. When you pay it and the payment appears in the bank feed, match it to the bill.
    Keep the paperwork

    To reclaim VAT, you need a valid VAT invoice or receipt. Attaching a photo or PDF to each bill or expense keeps it with the transaction. The QuickBooks phone app can photograph receipts for you.

    New suppliers appear on their own

    QuickBooks adds new suppliers it recognises from your bank feed automatically. If that creates a messy list, it can be turned off in the settings on the Bank transactions page.

    Lesson 11 of 13

    VAT and Making Tax Digital

    6 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Connect QuickBooks to HMRC
    • Check and submit a VAT return

    All VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and send VAT returns to HMRC using compatible software. QuickBooks Online is compatible, and can send your return directly.

    Step 1: connect to HMRC (once)

    1. Go to Taxes and select Set up MTD.
    2. Select Authorise now, then Continue.
    3. Sign in with the business’s HMRC Government Gateway user ID and password.
    4. Select Give permission. When it’s done, an MTD badge appears on the Taxes page.
    Every 18 months

    HMRC’s rules mean QuickBooks has to be re-authorised every 18 months. If a submission fails with an authorisation error, repeat the steps above.

    Step 2: check and submit a return

    1. Make sure every bank transaction for the period has been reviewed and the bank reconciled (lesson 12).
    2. Go to Taxes, then VAT, and open the Returns tab.
    3. Next to the return that’s due, select Prepare return.
    4. Use the VAT error checker from the drop-down to look for common mistakes, then review the figures.
    5. When you’re sure, tick the declaration and select Submit to HMRC. Keep the confirmation.
    6. When you pay HMRC, record the payment and match it in the bank feed.
    Once it’s submitted, it’s with HMRC

    A submitted return can’t simply be undone in QuickBooks. If Actua prepares your VAT returns, never submit one yourself; we’ll review and file it for you. VAT returns and payments are normally due one calendar month and seven days after the end of the VAT period.

    Lesson 12 of 13

    Reconciling your bank

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What reconciling proves
    • Reconcile to a £0.00 difference

    Reconciling means checking that QuickBooks agrees with your real bank statement. When it’s finished, the difference between the two must be £0.00. It’s the proof your books are right.

    1. First, deal with everything in For review for that account.
    2. Go to All apps, then Accounting, then Reconcile (older layout: Transactions, then Reconcile).
    3. Choose the account, then enter the ending balance and ending date from your statement. Some banks call it the closing balance or new balance.
    4. Select Start reconciling.
    5. Tick each transaction that appears on your statement. Leave it unticked if it isn’t on the statement yet.
    6. When the Difference shows £0.00, select Finish now.
    Not reaching £0.00?

    Select Edit info and check the ending balance and date you typed. Then look for a transaction that’s missing, duplicated, or the wrong amount. See “Something’s gone wrong” below.

    Reports that matter

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The reports worth knowing
    • Run, print and export a report
    ReportAnswers the question
    Profit and LossDid the business make money over this period?
    Balance SheetWhat does the business own and owe on this date?
    Aged receivables (who owes you)Which customers owe money, and how overdue is it?
    Aged payables (who you owe)Which suppliers are waiting to be paid?
    1. Select Reports on the left menu.
    2. Find the report by name, or type it into the report search box.
    3. Set the date range, such as “This month” or “Last financial year”, and run the report.
    4. Use the print or export buttons to save a PDF or a spreadsheet.
    Reports are only as good as the bookkeeping

    If transactions are still waiting in For review, or the bank isn’t reconciled, the reports will be wrong. Review and reconcile first, then report.

    Check yourself

    Eight quick questions

    Pick an answer to see whether you’re right, and why.

    Certificate

    Your certificate of completion

    Help

    Something’s gone wrong

    Select a problem to see the fix.

    My bank feed has stopped bringing in transactions
    1. Go to Bank transactions and look for a message on the account, such as a request to reconnect.
    2. Follow the prompt to sign in to your bank again and renew your permission.
    3. Remember pending transactions don’t come through until they’ve cleared at the bank.
    The same transaction appears twice

    On the For review tab, tick the duplicate and select Exclude. If both copies have already been added, ask your accountant before deleting anything.

    I added something with the wrong category

    Go to the Categorised tab, find the transaction and select Undo. It goes back to For review, where you can deal with it properly.

    An invoice still shows as unpaid, but the customer has paid

    The payment was probably added as income instead of matched. Find it on the Categorised tab, select Undo, then Match it to the invoice.

    My reconciliation won’t reach £0.00
    • Select Edit info and check the ending balance and ending date match your statement exactly.
    • Look for transactions on the statement that aren’t ticked, or that are missing from QuickBooks.
    • Look for duplicates, or amounts typed wrongly.
    • Still stuck? Save for later and ask your accountant. Don’t force it to finish.
    My VAT return won’t submit

    The most common cause is that QuickBooks needs re-authorising with HMRC (every 18 months). Go to Taxes and follow the MTD set-up steps in lesson 11 again. Also check the VAT registration number in QuickBooks matches HMRC’s records.

    I can’t find a page

    Type its name into the search bar at the top, such as “Reconcile” or “Chart of accounts”. Or open All apps. If it still isn’t there, your plan or your user permissions may not include it.

    Reference

    Bookkeeping words explained

    Accruals
    Recording income and costs when they’re invoiced or billed, not when cash moves. QuickBooks Online works this way.
    Aged receivables
    A report of who owes the business money, and for how long. Also called aged debtors.
    Bank feed
    The automatic link that brings bank transactions into QuickBooks.
    Bank rule
    An instruction telling QuickBooks how to categorise repeat transactions.
    Bill
    A supplier’s request for payment that you’ll pay later.
    Category
    A label for money in or out. Also called an account.
    Chart of accounts
    The full list of categories.
    Exclude
    Remove a downloaded transaction so it never reaches the books.
    Expense
    A cost you’ve already paid.
    Government Gateway
    The HMRC sign-in used to connect QuickBooks for VAT.
    Invoice
    A request for payment sent to a customer.
    Match
    Link a bank transaction to something already in QuickBooks, so it isn’t counted twice.
    MTD
    Making Tax Digital: HMRC’s rules for digital records and online returns.
    Reconcile
    Check QuickBooks agrees with the bank statement, to a £0.00 difference.
    Sales receipt
    A record of a sale paid for at the time.
    Supplier
    A business you buy from.
