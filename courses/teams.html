    Lesson 1 of 11

    What Teams actually is

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The three jobs Teams does
    • Five words you’ll see constantly

    Microsoft Teams is one app that does three jobs for a group of people.

    Talk in writing

    Send messages, like texting, to one person or a group.

    Talk face to face

    Video and voice calls, and online meetings booked for a set time.

    Share files

    Send documents and work on them together.

    Your workplace or organisation sets Teams up and gives you an account. Everyone in that organisation can then find and message each other.

    Five words you’ll see constantly

    WordWhat it means
    ChatA private conversation with one person or a small group you pick.
    TeamA bigger group, like a department. It has its own shared space.
    ChannelA topic room inside a team, such as “General” or “Rotas”.
    PostA message that starts a topic in a channel. Others reply underneath.
    MeetingA video call booked for a set time, with a link to join.
    Think of it like this

    A team is a building. Channels are rooms in the building. Chats are phone calls between people, whichever building they’re in.

    Getting Teams and signing in

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The three ways to use Teams
    • How to sign in safely

    All three ways show the same messages and meetings, so pick whichever suits you.

    WhereHow to get itBest for
    Computer appSearch the web for “Download Microsoft Teams”, choose Microsoft’s own website, and install it.Everyday use at a desk
    Web browserGo to teams.microsoft.com in Edge or Chrome. Nothing to install.Borrowed or shared computers
    Phone or tabletInstall “Microsoft Teams” from the App Store or Google Play.Messages and calls on the move

    Signing in

    1. Open Teams. It asks for an email address.
    2. Type the email address your organisation gave you, not a personal Gmail or Hotmail address unless you were told to use it.
    3. Type your password and select Sign in.
    4. You may be asked to approve the sign-in on your phone, or type a code sent by text. This is normal security, called two-step verification.
    Can’t sign in?

    Only your organisation’s IT person or administrator can reset your Teams password. Microsoft can’t do it for you.

    Lesson 3 of 11

    Finding your way around

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The four areas of the Teams screen
    • How to find anything with search
    1. The strip down the far left picks what you’re looking at: Activity, Chat, Calendar, Calls, OneDrive and Apps. Select one and everything to its right changes.
    2. The middle column is a list. In Chat it shows your conversations; in Calendar it becomes your diary.
    3. The big area on the right shows whatever you selected in the list, such as a conversation.
    4. The bar along the top has the search box and your picture.

    Go back to the pretend screen and select each part to see it in action.

    Can’t see a separate Teams button?

    Current Teams shows your chats, teams and channels together under Chat. You can switch to separate Chat and Teams views in Settings. Your organisation can also add or hide buttons on the left strip; select ••• there to find hidden ones.

    Search finds almost anything

    Type a person’s name, a word from an old message, or a file name into the search box at the top. Shortcut: Ctrl + E on Windows, Cmd + E on a Mac.

    Lesson 4 of 11

    Sending your first message

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Start a chat and send a message
    • React, reply, edit and delete
    1. Select Chat on the left strip.
    2. At the top of the list, select the New message button (a small pencil).
    3. In the To: box, start typing a person’s name and pick them from the list that appears. Add more names for a group chat.
    4. Click into the message box at the bottom and type your message.
    5. Press Enter or select the paper-plane button to send.
    1. Type your message here.
    2. Emoji: add a smiley or other picture.
    3. Paperclip: attach a file (see lesson 10).
    4. Plus: extra options, such as formatting text.
    5. Paper plane: send.

    Reacting, replying, editing and deleting

    • React: hover your mouse over a message (on a phone, press and hold it) and pick a thumbs-up or another reaction. A quick way to say “seen it” without typing.
    • Reply to one message: hover over it, select •••, then Reply. Your answer shows a quote of the original, so everyone knows what you mean.
    • Edit or delete your own message: hover over it, select ••• (More options), then Edit or Delete.
    Bold names mean unread

    In the list, a name in bold has messages you haven’t opened yet.

    Lesson 5 of 11

    Teams and channels

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • How channels differ from chats
    • Posting, replying and @mentions

    Someone in your organisation adds you to a team. You’ll find it in the Chat list under Teams and channels. Select the team’s name to show its channels; every team starts with a General channel.

    Channels work differently from chats. Instead of one long stream, a channel is a set of posts, each with its own replies underneath. This keeps separate topics apart.

    1. Start a post (sometimes worded “Post in channel”): use this only for a brand-new topic.
    2. Reply: use this to answer an existing post. Your answer stays tucked under it.

    Getting someone’s attention with @

    1. While typing, type the @ symbol.
    2. Start typing the person’s name and pick them from the list. Their name turns into a highlighted link.
    3. Send. They get a notification that you mentioned them.
    The golden rule of channels

    Answering a post? Always select Reply. Starting a new post to answer an old one scatters the conversation.

    Lesson 6 of 11

    Joining a meeting

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Three ways into a meeting
    • Using the preview screen and the lobby
    • From an email invitation: select the join link in the invitation.
    • From Calendar: select Calendar on the left, select the meeting, then select Join.
    • From a chat: if a meeting card appears in a chat, as on the pretend screen, select its Join button.

    Before you go in, Teams shows a preview screen so you can check how you look and sound.

    1. Camera switch: on shows your face, off shows your initials.
    2. Microphone switch: off means nobody can hear you.
    3. Audio choice: leave it on Computer audio unless told otherwise. The options depend on your organisation.
    4. Join now: select it when you’re ready.
    “Someone will let you in shortly”

    You’re in the waiting area, called the lobby. The organiser, or someone they’ve chosen, has to admit you. Just wait; you haven’t done anything wrong.

    No account? You can usually still join

    If someone outside your organisation sends you a meeting link, opening it normally lets you join in a web browser as a guest. Type your name when asked. Some organisations restrict this.

    Lesson 7 of 11

    The buttons inside a meeting

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What every meeting button does
    • Good meeting manners

    Once you’re in, a row of buttons sits along the top of the meeting window.

    Simplified drawing. The exact order of buttons can vary between versions of Teams.

    ButtonWhat it does
    MicMutes or unmutes you. A line through the microphone means nobody can hear you.
    CameraTurns your video on or off.
    LeaveLeaves the meeting. The others carry on without you. You can rejoin the same way you joined.
    RaisePuts a small hand by your name, so the host knows you want to speak. Select it again to lower it.
    ReactShows a quick emoji, like a thumbs-up or applause, over your video.
    ChatOpens a written chat alongside the meeting. Handy for sharing links.
    PeopleShows who’s in the meeting.
    ShareShows your screen, or one window, to everyone. Select Stop sharing when you’ve finished.
    ViewChanges how people are laid out on your screen. Only affects you.
    More (•••)Extra options, including settings for your speaker and microphone, and background effects.
    Good meeting manners

    Stay muted while others talk, and unmute to speak. It stops background noise drowning out the speaker.

    Lesson 8 of 11

    Setting up your own meeting

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Book a meeting for later
    • Start a meeting straight away

    A meeting for later

    1. Select Calendar on the left strip.
    2. Select New meeting near the top right. In some versions it just says New, or you can select an empty time slot.
    3. If you see a Teams meeting switch, make sure it’s on. That’s what adds the join link.
    4. Give the meeting a title, such as “Monday catch-up”.
    5. In the attendees box, type each person’s name or email address and pick them from the list.
    6. Set the date, start time and end time, and optionally add a short agenda.
    7. Select Send (some versions say Save). Everyone gets an invitation with the join link.

    You can also book a meeting from inside a chat: look for Schedule meeting at the top of the conversation. Everyone in the chat is added for you.

    A meeting right now

    1. In Calendar, select Meet now.
    2. Start the meeting, then share the link it gives you, or add people from the People button once you’re in.
    It’s the same diary as Outlook

    If your organisation uses Outlook for email, meetings you book in Teams appear in your Outlook calendar too, and the other way round.

    Lesson 9 of 11

    Making a call

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Ring someone from a chat
    • Answer or decline a call

    A call is an instant, unbooked conversation, like ringing someone on the phone.

    1. Open your chat with the person or group.
    2. Look at the top right of the conversation. Select the video camera for a video call, or the phone for a voice-only call.
    3. Their Teams rings. When they answer, you’ll see the same buttons as in a meeting (lesson 7).

    Receiving a call

    A small box pops up, usually at the bottom right of your screen, showing who’s calling. Choose the video button to answer with your camera on, the phone button to answer with voice only, or the red button to decline.

    Calling ordinary phone numbers

    Teams can only ring landlines and mobiles if your organisation has paid for that. If you can’t see a dial pad under Calls, you can still call other Teams users.

    Lesson 10 of 11

    Sharing files

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Send a file in a chat or channel
    • Find shared files later, and share safely
    1. Select the paperclip in the message box. On some versions, select + first, then Attach file.
    2. Choose Upload from this device, then find the file on your computer and open it.
    3. Wait for it to finish uploading, add a message if you like, then send.

    Find files later

    • In a chat or channel, look along the top for a Files or Shared tab. It lists everything shared there.
    • Select OneDrive on the left strip to see files you’ve shared or that were shared with you. Some versions call this Files.
    Everyone edits the same copy

    When you share a Word, Excel or PowerPoint file in Teams, people open the same file rather than separate copies. Changes save automatically.

    Think before you share

    Anything you share can be seen by everyone in that chat or channel. Never post client financial details, bank details or passwords in a channel.

    Lesson 11 of 11

    Your status and notifications

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Set your status and a status message
    • Control notifications

    The small coloured dot on your picture tells others whether it’s a good time to message you. Teams changes it automatically, for example to Busy when you’re in a meeting, but you can set it yourself.

    1. Select your picture (or initials) at the top right.
    2. Select the line showing your current status, such as “Available”.
    3. Pick a new status from the list.
    4. Optional: select Set status message to add a note like “On leave until Monday”.
    StatusUse it when
    BusyYou’re concentrating but still want pop-ups.
    Do not disturbYou don’t want pop-ups at all. It stays on until you change it, unless you set a duration.
    Be right backYou’re stepping away briefly. Teams never sets this for you.
    Appear offlineYou want to look signed out. You still receive messages and notifications.

    Notifications

    • Activity (the bell) collects everything aimed at you: mentions, replies and reactions. A red number shows how many are new.
    • Too many pop-ups? Select ••• next to your picture, then Settings, then the notifications section, and turn down what you don’t need.
    • To silence one busy chat, hover over it in the list, select •••, then Mute.

    Help

    Something’s gone wrong

    Select a problem to see the fix.

    Nobody can hear me
    1. Look at the Mic button. If it has a line through it, select it to unmute.
    2. Still nothing? Select ••• (More), open the audio or device settings, and pick the right microphone. Try each one if you’re unsure.
    3. Using a headset? Check it isn’t muted by its own switch on the cable or earpiece.
    I can’t hear anyone
    1. Check your computer’s volume isn’t turned down or muted.
    2. Select ••• (More), open the audio or device settings, and choose the right speaker.
    3. Leave and rejoin the meeting. This fixes more than you’d expect.
    My camera shows a black screen
    • Check for a physical cover or slider over the camera lens.
    • Close other apps that might be using the camera, such as Zoom or the Camera app.
    • In the browser version, look for a camera symbol in the address bar and choose Allow.
    • In the device settings, check the right camera is selected.
    I pressed Leave by accident

    No harm done. Rejoin exactly as you joined the first time: from the email link, from Calendar, or from the meeting card in the chat.

    I’m stuck on “someone will let you in”

    You’re in the lobby. The organiser has to admit you. If you’ve waited more than a few minutes, contact the organiser another way, such as a text or phone call.

    I can’t find an old message

    Select the search box at the top (or press Ctrl + E) and type a word you remember from the message, or the person’s name.

    Teams looks different from these pictures

    Microsoft updates Teams regularly, and organisations can hide or add buttons. Button names stay mostly the same, so look for the words rather than the exact position.

    Reference

    Teams words explained

    @mention
    Typing @ and a name to notify that person directly.
    Activity
    Your notifications feed, under the bell.
    Channel
    A topic room inside a team. Messages there are posts with replies.
    Chat
    A private conversation with people you choose.
    Guest
    Someone from outside your organisation who’s been let into a team or meeting.
    Lobby
    The waiting area before the organiser lets you into a meeting.
    Meeting
    A scheduled video call with a join link.
    Mute
    Turning your microphone off, or silencing notifications from a chat.
    Organiser
    The person who set up a meeting. They can admit people and end it.
    Post
    A message that starts a new topic in a channel.
    Reaction
    A quick emoji response to a message, like a thumbs-up.
    Screen share
    Showing what’s on your screen to others in a call.
    Status
    The coloured dot showing whether you’re available, busy or away.
    Team
    A group of people with a shared space made up of channels.
