    Lesson 1 of 8

    What running a payroll means

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What an employer must do
    • The words you’ll hear

    When a business takes on staff, it becomes an employer. That means working out each person’s pay, taking off the right deductions, paying HMRC, and reporting it all every time people are paid.

    1. Register as an employer with HMRC before the first payday. HMRC gives you an employer PAYE reference.
    2. Use HMRC-recognised payroll software, such as Sage 50 Payroll or Sage Payroll online.
    3. Each payday, work out pay and deductions, give payslips, and report to HMRC.
    4. Pay HMRC what you’ve deducted, plus employer costs, every month.
    WordMeaning
    PAYEPay As You Earn: taking Income Tax and National Insurance from wages before people are paid
    RTIReal Time Information: reporting payroll to HMRC on or before each payday
    Gross payPay before deductions
    Net payWhat the employee actually receives
    Tax codeTells payroll how much tax-free pay someone gets, such as 1257L

    From gross pay to net pay

    6 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The deductions from an employee’s pay
    • What the employer pays on top

    Taken from the employee’s pay

    Deduction2026 to 2027 (England, Wales, Northern Ireland)
    Income TaxMost people get £12,570 a year tax-free (tax code 1257L). Then 20% basic rate, 40% higher rate, 45% additional rate. Scotland has its own rates.
    Employee National Insurance8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 a year, then 2% above that
    Workplace pensionUsually at least 5% of qualifying earnings (see lesson 7)
    Student loanOnly if HMRC tells you; the rate depends on the plan

    Paid by the employer on top

    Cost2026 to 2027
    Employer National Insurance15% on earnings above £5,000 a year (about £417 a month)
    Employment AllowanceEligible employers can reduce their employer National Insurance bill by up to £10,500 a year. Not available if the only employee is a director.
    Employer pensionAt least 3% of qualifying earnings

    Lesson 3 of 8

    Taking on a new starter

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What to collect
    • The checks you must do
    1. Check the person has the right to work in the UK before they start, and keep a copy of the evidence.
    2. Ask for their P45 from their last job. If they don’t have one, they complete HMRC’s starter checklist.
    3. Collect their name, address, date of birth, National Insurance number and bank details.
    4. Add them to the payroll software. It works out the right tax code to use until HMRC sends one.
    5. Assess them for pension auto-enrolment (lesson 7).
    6. Report them on the first FPS when you pay them.
    Right to work matters

    Employing someone without checking their right to work can lead to a large civil penalty. Do the check before the first day, every time.

    Lesson 4 of 8

    Each pay run, and reporting to HMRC

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The FPS and EPS
    • When HMRC must be paid
    WhatWhen
    Full Payment Submission (FPS): tells HMRC what each employee was paid and what was deductedOn or before each payday
    Payslips: every employee must get oneOn or before payday
    Employer Payment Summary (EPS): to claim the Employment Allowance, reclaim statutory pay, or tell HMRC you paid nobodyBy the 19th of the following tax month
    Pay HMRC: tax, National Insurance and student loansBy the 22nd of the following tax month if paying electronically (the 19th by post)
    Tax months

    Payroll uses tax months running from the 6th of one month to the 5th of the next. Pay made on 28 October falls in the tax month 6 October to 5 November, so HMRC must be paid by 22 November.

    Payslip rules

    Payslips must show gross pay, each deduction and net pay. If pay varies with hours worked, the payslip must show the hours.

    Lesson 5 of 8

    Minimum wage

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • The 2026 to 2027 rates
    AgeHourly rate from April 2026
    21 and over (National Living Wage)£12.71
    18 to 20£10.85
    16 to 17£8.00
    Apprentices (under 19, or in the first year)£8.00
    Watch for hidden underpayment

    Unpaid time such as compulsory training or travel between jobs, deductions for uniforms, or salary sacrifice can take pay below the minimum. HMRC enforces the minimum wage and names employers who underpay.

    Rates change every April

    The minimum wage goes up each April. Check every employee’s hourly rate then.

    Lesson 6 of 8

    Statutory pay

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Sick pay from day one
    • Family-related pay
    Payment2026 to 2027
    Statutory Sick Pay (SSP)£123.25 a week, or 80% of average weekly earnings if lower. Payable from the first day of sickness, with no minimum earnings level.
    Statutory Maternity, Paternity, Adoption, Shared Parental, Bereavement and Neonatal Care Pay£194.32 a week, or 90% of average weekly earnings if lower. Statutory Maternity and Adoption Pay are 90% of average weekly earnings for the first 6 weeks.
    New since April 2026

    SSP used to start on the fourth day of sickness and only applied to people earning at least the lower earnings limit. Both rules were removed from April 2026.

    Reclaiming

    Employers can usually reclaim most family-related statutory pay from HMRC through the EPS. Small employers can reclaim more. Your payroll software works this out.

    Lesson 7 of 8

    Workplace pensions

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Who must be enrolled
    • Minimum contributions
    Threshold2026 to 2027
    Earnings trigger: must be automatically enrolled if aged 22 to State Pension age and earning more than£10,000 a year
    Qualifying earnings: contributions are usually worked out on earnings between£6,240 and £50,270 a year
    Minimum contributions8% in total, with at least 3% from the employer
    1. Assess every worker each pay run. The software does this for you.
    2. Enrol eligible workers and write to them within six weeks.
    3. Employees can opt out; you must not encourage them to.
    4. Pay contributions to the pension scheme on time.
    5. Every three years, re-enrol people who opted out, and re-declare to The Pensions Regulator.
    Your declaration of compliance

    Every new employer must complete a declaration of compliance with The Pensions Regulator, even if nobody needs enrolling. Missing it can lead to fines.

    Lesson 8 of 8

    Leavers and year end

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What to do when someone leaves
    • The year-end deadlines

    When someone leaves

    1. Pay their final wages, including any holiday they’ve built up and not taken.
    2. Include their leaving date on that FPS.
    3. Give them a P45.

    Year end (the tax year ends 5 April)

    DeadlineWhat
    Last payday before 5 AprilFinal FPS of the year, marked as the final submission (or a final EPS if needed)
    31 MayGive every employee still with you on 5 April a P60
    6 JulyReport taxable benefits and expenses on P11D forms, unless you payroll them
    Coming in April 2027

    HMRC plans to make reporting most benefits in kind through payroll (“payrolling”) compulsory from April 2027. Your accountant can help you prepare.

    Help

    Common questions

    Select a question to see the answer.

    An employee’s tax code looks wrong

    Use the code HMRC sends you. If the employee thinks it’s wrong, they should contact HMRC; you can’t change it yourself without an HMRC notice.

    I forgot to send an FPS on payday

    Send it as soon as possible with a late reporting reason. Repeated late FPS submissions can lead to penalties.

    Can directors be on the payroll?

    Yes. Directors have special National Insurance rules worked out on an annual basis. Your payroll software and accountant handle this.

    Someone wants to be paid cash in hand

    All employees must be run through payroll. Paying cash doesn’t change the tax, National Insurance, minimum wage or pension rules.

    Reference

    Words explained

    EPS
    Employer Payment Summary: monthly report for allowances, reclaims or no payments.
    Employment Allowance
    Up to £10,500 a year off employer National Insurance for eligible employers.
    FPS
    Full Payment Submission: the payday report to HMRC.
    P45
    Given to a leaver, showing pay and tax to date.
    P60
    Year-end summary of pay and tax, given by 31 May.
    P11D
    Report of taxable benefits and expenses, due 6 July.
    Qualifying earnings
    The pay band pension contributions are usually based on.
    RTI
    Real Time Information: reporting payroll to HMRC each payday.
    Tax month
    The 6th of one month to the 5th of the next.
