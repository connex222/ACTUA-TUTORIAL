    What Self Assessment is, and who files

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What a tax return does
    • Common reasons you need one

    Self Assessment is how HMRC collects Income Tax from people whose tax isn’t all taken automatically through wages or pensions. Once a year, you (or your accountant) send a tax return showing your income, and you pay what’s owed.

    You’ll usually need to file if you…
    Are self-employed as a sole trader and earned more than £1,000 from it
    Are a partner in a business partnership
    Received more than £1,000 from renting out property
    Had other untaxed income, such as some savings, investment or foreign income, or capital gains to report
    Were sent a notice to file by HMRC
    Not sure?

    GOV.UK has a short online checker: search for “Check if you need to send a Self Assessment tax return”. Your accountant can also tell you.

    The tax year

    The UK tax year runs from 6 April to the following 5 April. A return for the 2025 to 2026 tax year covers income from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026.

    Lesson 2 of 7

    Registering, and your UTR

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • When to register
    • What a UTR is

    If you need to file for the first time, you must register with HMRC by 5 October after the end of the tax year. For example, if you started trading in the 2025 to 2026 tax year, register by 5 October 2026.

    1. Register on GOV.UK (search “Register for Self Assessment”).
    2. HMRC sends you a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR): a 10-digit number. It can take a couple of weeks to arrive.
    3. Keep your UTR safe. You and your accountant will need it for every return.
    Late registration

    If you register late and owe tax, HMRC can charge a penalty for failing to tell them on time. Register as soon as you know you need to.

    The key dates

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • Every deadline in the Self Assessment year

    Here are the dates for the 2025 to 2026 tax year return, the one being prepared now.

    DateWhat’s due
    5 October 2026Register, if you’re new to Self Assessment
    31 October 2026Paper tax return
    30 December 2026Online return, if you want tax under £3,000 collected through your tax code (only if you also have a job or pension paid through PAYE)
    31 January 2027Online tax return, the balance of tax owed for 2025 to 2026, and the first payment on account for 2026 to 2027
    31 July 2027The second payment on account for 2026 to 2027
    31 January is two deadlines

    It’s the deadline for filing the return and for paying. Missing it starts two separate sets of penalties.

    Lesson 4 of 7

    Payments on account

    5 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What payments on account are
    • Why your first January bill can be a shock

    Payments on account are advance payments towards next year’s tax bill. HMRC asks for them if your last Self Assessment bill was £1,000 or more, unless 80% or more of your tax was already collected at source (for example through PAYE).

    1. Each payment on account is half of last year’s bill.
    2. The first is due 31 January, and the second 31 July.
    3. After the year ends, if you owe more, you pay a balancing payment the following 31 January. If you’ve paid too much, it’s credited or refunded.
    Example: a first year in business

    Sam’s tax bill for 2025 to 2026 is £4,000, and it’s their first year. On 31 January 2027 Sam pays the £4,000 plus a first payment on account of £2,000 for 2026 to 2027: £6,000. Then on 31 July 2027, a further £2,000.

    Income falling?

    If you expect next year’s bill to be lower, you can ask HMRC to reduce your payments on account. If you reduce them too far, you’ll pay interest on the shortfall, so ask your accountant first.

    Put money aside

    Setting aside a fixed share of every payment you receive into a separate savings account makes January far less painful.

    Penalties and interest

    4 minutes

    In this lesson
    • What a late return costs
    • What late payment costs

    Late filing

    How latePenalty
    1 day£100, even if you owe no tax
    3 months£10 a day, for up to 90 days (up to £900)
    6 months£300 or 5% of the tax due, whichever is higher
    12 monthsAnother £300 or 5% of the tax due, whichever is higher

    Late payment

    How latePenalty
    30 days5% of the tax unpaid
    6 monthsAnother 5% of the tax unpaid
    12 monthsAnother 5% of the tax unpaid

    HMRC also charges interest on late tax from the day after it was due.

    If you’re in Making Tax Digital

    People in MTD for Income Tax have a points-based system for late submissions instead. The MTD course covers it.

    Can’t pay in full?

    File on time anyway, then contact HMRC about a payment plan. Filing late as well only adds more penalties.

    Working with your accountant

    3 minutes

    In this lesson
    • A calm timetable
    WhenWhat to do
    April to JuneThe tax year has ended. Gather your records and send them over.
    SummerYour accountant prepares the return and tells you what you owe.
    AutumnCheck and approve the return. Plan for January’s payment.
    By 31 JanuaryReturn filed; pay what’s due.
    By 31 JulyPay the second payment on account, if you have one.
    Don’t wait for January

    Accountants are at their busiest in January. Sending records early gives time to spot savings and fix problems, and you’ll know your bill months in advance.

      Why is my January bill so much bigger than I expected?

      In your first year with a bill of £1,000 or more, January includes that year’s tax plus the first payment on account for the next year, so it can be around one and a half times your bill.

      I’ve lost my UTR

      It’s on letters from HMRC and in your HMRC online account or the HMRC app. Your accountant may also have it on file.

      I stopped being self-employed. Do I still file?

      Usually yes, for the tax year you stopped in. Tell HMRC you’ve stopped, and speak to your accountant about closing down properly.

      Can I pay in instalments?

      You can pay towards your bill early, and HMRC offers a budget payment plan. If you can’t pay on time, contact HMRC about Time to Pay before the deadline.

      Reference

      Words explained

      Balancing payment
      The amount still owed after payments on account, due on 31 January.
      Payment on account
      An advance payment towards next year’s bill, each half of last year’s.
      Self Assessment
      HMRC’s system for collecting tax not taken automatically.
      Tax year
      6 April to 5 April.
      Trading allowance
      £1,000 of self-employment income each year that can be tax-free.
      UTR
      Unique Taxpayer Reference: your 10-digit Self Assessment number.
